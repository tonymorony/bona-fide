A small Deposit shows good faith

Maintainers are drowning in low-effort PRs and issues, and now AI agents open them too. Closing the repo to newcomers keeps out good work along with the noise.

Bona Fide asks Contributors you don't know yet for a small refundable Deposit instead: 1 USDC by default. Good-faith work gets it back in full.

What happens to a 1 USDC Deposit

  • Merged or accepted

    Bona Fide sends the Refund.

    1 USDC refunded

  • No decision before the Timeout

    7 days by default. After it, Bona Fide sends the Refund.

    1 USDC refunded

  • Bad faith, signed by a Maintainer

    0.50 USDC funds open-source maintainers (Protocol Guild). It's never more than half.

    0.50 USDC refunded

Nobody else gets any of it: not the Maintainers, and not Bona Fide.

How it works

Maintainers set it up once. Contributors and their agents deposit when a repo asks.

Maintainers

  1. Install the Bona Fide App on your repo.
  2. Commit .github/bona-fide.yml with your wallet address. The defaults: 1 USDC, a 50% forfeit share and a 7-day Timeout.
  3. Sign once with that wallet to prove it's yours. Deposits start 24 hours later.
Read the Maintainer guide

Contributors

  1. Open your PR or issue as usual. If the repo asks for a Deposit, Bona Fide links you to the deposit page.
  2. Deposit with one signature, and Bona Fide sends it for you. Or approve and deposit from your own wallet, in two transactions.
  3. Get it back in full when your work is merged or accepted, or when the Timeout ends.
Read how Deposits work

AI agents

  1. Add the Bona Fide MCP server to your agent. It reads a repo's terms and prepares the Deposit.
  2. Your agent signs with its own wallet, such as a Circle agent wallet, and Bona Fide's relay sends it.
  3. The server never holds keys, and it refuses any Deposit above your limit: 5 USDC by default.
Read the agent guide

Who never needs a Deposit

The check passes at once, with no Deposit, for:

  • Collaborators with write access to the repo
  • Contributors vouched for in VOUCHED.td
  • Anyone with a merged PR in the repo
  • Allowed bots, such as Dependabot and Renovate
  • PRs and issues a Maintainer labels bona-fide:waive
  • PRs and issues opened before Bona Fide started in the repo

Nobody can take the money

An escrow contract on Arc holds every Deposit, and nobody can change its rules.

No owner, no upgrades

The escrow is immutable: it has no owner, no admin and no upgrade path. Nobody can pause it.

The Keeper can only give back

The App's signer, the Keeper, can only refund a Deposit to the wallet that paid it. When it relays a Forfeit, at most half goes to the forfeit router.

Forfeits need the Maintainer's signature

Only the Maintainer's own wallet can sign one, for that one Deposit, and only before its Timeout ends.

The Timeout refunds automatically

If nobody decides in time, the Deposit is refunded in full. The Keeper sends it, and anyone else can too.

Built on Arc

Every Deposit, Refund and Forfeit happens on-chain, in USDC.

Deposits and fees in USDC

Deposits and network fees are both paid in USDC on Arc, so Contributors need no other token. Arc settles each Deposit in under a second, so the check turns green within seconds.

Forfeits fund open-source maintainers

Forfeited USDC waits in the forfeit router until anyone sends it on. It crosses to Base through Circle's CCTP, into the vesting contract of Protocol Guild.

Questions

Why USDC?

A Deposit should keep its value while the escrow holds it. USDC is a digital dollar, and on Arc it also pays the network fee, so Contributors need no other token.

What if a Maintainer is malicious?

They can't profit from it. A Forfeit takes at most half a Deposit, needs the Maintainer's signature before the Timeout ends, and sends that half only to the forfeit router, which funds open-source maintainers (Protocol Guild). The rest goes back to the Contributor. Nobody else can receive any of it.

What happens at the Timeout?

If nobody has decided by then, 7 days after the Deposit by default, it's refunded in full. The Keeper sends the Refund on its own, and anyone else can send it too. After the Timeout, nobody can forfeit it.

Does it cost anything?

Bona Fide charges no fees. Arc's network fee is paid in USDC. A one-signature Deposit costs the Contributor nothing: Bona Fide relays it and pays the fee. Two steps from your own wallet cost two small network fees.
